relative occupancy of water-mediated vs direct non-bonded interactions between each protein residue and the ligand. 
A value of R ≃ 0 would mean that either a non-bonded interaction is not frequently formed between this residue and 
the ligand or that the interaction is formed equally as a direct and water-mediated interaction. A value of R ≃ -1 would 
mean that there is a water -mediated interaction formed for close to 100% of all trajectories and that there is no 
significant period of the trajectory for which a bonded interaction is formed between this same residue and the ligand. 
(A) There are numerous residues in the receptor for whic h R<-0.7 in both mandipropamid and WIN. For PYR1mandi

an R score between 0.45 and 0.7 and also formed water mediated interactions for >95% of trajectories.

Figure S4: Representative strong and stable h-bonds establishes thresholds for water-mediated h-bonds. 
We have included control residues with the same residue identity as each residue determined to have a primarily water-
mediated h-bond with the ligand (defined as the “reference residue”). The solvent-exposed control is a residue as the 
same type as the reference residue but located in a solvent exposed position on the receptor. The pocket control residue 
is a residue the same type as the reference and located within the binding pocket, but with a combined <10% contact 
with the ligand from either direct or water -mediated interactions. (A) The control residues from PYR1 mandi have a 
combined mean H–acceptor distance of 1.81 Å and 93% of the control bonds have a mean distance < 2.5 Å. Also, 
80% of these residues have a standard deviation < 0.45 Å. (B) The control residues for PYR1 WIN have a combined 
mean H–acceptor distance of 2.42 Å and 89% of the control bonds have a mean distance < 2.5 Å. Also, 89% of these 
residues have a standard deviation < 0.45 Å. This da ta allows increased confidence in the thresholds established for 
use dividing the water mediated h-bonds into those which are strong and stable and those which are not.

Figure S6: Salt bridge formation is significantly impacted by mutation. We plot the mean occupancy of 
three salt-bridges which form across the binding pocket as well as the h -bond which forms between R59 and 
the ligand of both the WT and E141Y variants. There is an inverse relationship between the formation of the 
R59—E94 salt -bridge and the R59 —E/Y141 salt bridge in the WT complex. The formation of a salt bridge 
between R59 and the ligand appears to be central to the stabilization of the ligand within the binding pocket prior to 
HAB1 secondary binding. The R59–ligand salt bridge is disrupted by the E141Y mutation to PYR1mandi. The absence 
of the R59–ligand salt bridge is strongly correlated to the absence of the R59 -E/Y141 salt bridge which is disrupted 
directly by the E141Y mutation. In the absence of a salt-bridge between R59-E/Y141 R59 instead forms a salt-bridge 
with E94 which is also consistent with the salt-bridge structure found in Apo PYR1mandi.

Figure S7. E141Y Mutation Maintains a Stable Binding Complex. 
Here we plot the heavy atom RMSD relative to the centroid of each trajectory to compare the relative protein 
stability. We observe that the WT protein becomes more flexible with the removal of HAB1 from the complex, 
while the opposite effect is observed in the E141Y mutant variant. With the E141Y mutation, mandipropamid binds 
to a distinct location compared to binding in WT PYR1. This new binding location is not conducive to the further 
binding of HAB1. Thus, if we initialize the mutant PYR1 bound to HAB1 the ligand is trapped and forces the 
protein to reorganize around the ligand in a non-physical manner, which results in an unstable protein structure. For 
this reason, the insights into the conformational ensemble of mutant bound complex were taken from simulations 
which did not include HAB1.

Figure S11: Charge Neutral Mutations Disrupt Ligand Binding. 
(A) The 59 – 141 salt-bridge is formed in both the WT (blue) and R59E/E141R (green) variants as observed in MD, 
but not the R59Q/E141Q (peach) charge reversal variant (zoomed-in view in B). The formation of this charge 
reversed salt-bridge does not guarantee the formation of a stable h-bond with either residue 59 or 141, unlike the 
wild type (C). In the zoomed-in view one can see more clearly that while the R59E/E141R variant does not result in 
a complete reorientation of residue 59 like the R59Q/E141Q variant, the h-bond is shifted by ~3A relative to the 
acceptor on the ligand. A 3A reorientation of the ligand would significantly change the other non-bonded contacts 
the ligand is able the form. This suggests that while this mutation significantly disrupts binding for Mandi that may 
not be the case for a different ligand. The complete destruction of the salt-bridge in the R59Q/E141Q variant 
suggests that to rescue ligand binding the ligand would need to be chemically more similar to WIN which is able to 
rescue binding through the formation of water-mediated h-bonds.
